Experience is not the opposite of reinvention.

Thirty years of solving problems. Then I chose new problems.

My path into technology did not begin with a straight line from high school to a software company. It came through industrial systems, electrical work, maintenance, process controls, leadership, setbacks, school, and the decision to keep going.

The through-line

Troubleshooting never changed.

The tools changed. The environment changed. The core habit—observe carefully, isolate the problem, understand the system, and fix what actually failed—did not.

Industrial career

Electrical, mechanical, controls, and production systems

Decades in industrial environments developed practical troubleshooting, maintenance, process-control, team, and operations experience.

Networking foundation

Cisco and network operations coursework

Earlier networking studies built a foundation in IP addressing, Ethernet, routing, switching, LAN/WAN technologies, and network troubleshooting.

Age 56

Back to college—and this time all in

I returned to school with a different level of focus. Cybersecurity, programming, databases, systems, and networking became more than subjects; they became the direction of the next career.

Today

Information systems + hands-on infrastructure

I’m continuing a B.S. in Information Systems while building and administering a homelab that gives the coursework somewhere real to land.

Education

Formal learning, stacked over time.

These programs connect industrial technology, networking, cybersecurity, programming, and information systems rather than replacing one chapter with another.

Coastal Carolina University

B.S. Information Systems — in progress

Current studies extending the technical foundation into enterprise systems, software, analytics, and information technology.

Horry-Georgetown Technical College

A.A.S. Cybersecurity

Networking, security, firewalls, secure LAN/DMZ design, incident response, digital forensics, ethical hacking, and cyber-range experience.

North Seattle College

A.A.S. Energy & Process Controls

Industrial power, controls, PLCs, motor controls, electronics, automation, robotics, and related IT foundations.

York Technical College

Networking studies

Cisco routers and switches, IP addressing, routing, LAN/WAN concepts, protocols, and hands-on troubleshooting.

What I bring forward

Transferable means transferable.

Industrial experience taught me to work around real consequences, communicate with people under pressure, make changes carefully, and resist the temptation to replace parts before understanding the failure. Those habits translate unusually well to systems administration and cybersecurity.

What changed

I became a better student with age.

Returning to college showed me that the assumptions we make about age and learning are often wrong. I spend more time digging into the “why,” and I enjoy building outside the assignment until the concept becomes something I can actually use.
