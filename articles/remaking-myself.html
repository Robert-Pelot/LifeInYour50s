Remaking Myself: A Journey of Perseverance

By Robert Pelot  •  February 15, 2025
↗ ↗ ↗

Different flights. Same target. Keep aiming.

Reading Frederick Douglass's Self-Made Men has given me a profound understanding of perseverance and the importance of constant self-reinvention. A part of the writing that stands out to me deeply is the metaphor he uses about a thousand arrows shot from the same point, all aiming at the same target. Douglass describes the arrows as united in aim but divided in flight: some travel too high or too low, some drift right or left, and only a few hit the mark.

This speaks volumes to me because it mirrors my own journey—one of continual reinvention. Like those arrows, I feel as though I’ve made, and remade, myself countless times in pursuit of my goals, but I still haven’t hit my mark. There have been many missteps and redirections along the way, and while I haven’t yet reached the exact point I’m aiming for, I understand now that this is a natural part of the process.

The Process of Reinvention

What I take away from Douglass's words is the reminder that it's okay if things don’t always go as planned. It’s part of the journey. Life is full of trial and error, but each attempt, even if it doesn’t hit the target, brings me closer to understanding what works and what doesn’t. I may not always hit the mark right away, but I am constantly moving forward, learning, adjusting, and aiming again. Every effort is part of the growth and evolution of the person I’m becoming.

Persistence Over Perfection

So, I’ll keep aiming, keep remaking myself, and keep pursuing my goals—no matter how many times I have to try. Success isn't about perfection; it's about persistence and continual growth.

And here am I—remaking myself once again.
