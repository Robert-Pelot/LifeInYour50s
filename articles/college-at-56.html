The Journey: Going Back to College at 56

By Robert Pelot  •  December 22, 2024

At 56, many people start thinking about winding down their careers or enjoying the fruits of years of hard work. But for me, it was the perfect time to change gears and head back to school. My decision wasn’t impulsive or easy, but it was driven by a mix of personal, professional, and physical reasons. This post is my way of sharing why I chose this path—and why anyone, no matter their age, can benefit from embracing education later in life.

The Catalyst: Time for a Change

For decades, I worked in factories as a millwright and electrician. The physical demands of the job were taxing on my body, and I began to feel the effects of years of hard labor. As I noticed my body showing signs of wear and tear, I knew it was time to make a change. One significant event solidified this decision for me: about three years ago, I fell from a machine at work and landed directly on my head. While I was lucky to survive, it was a wake-up call. It reminded me that I needed to take care of my body and stop doing work that was physically demanding. This was the point where I realized it was time to transition to something different—something that would preserve my health and open new doors for my future.

A Past Connection with Learning

I had always been interested in technology and learning new skills, even before this decision. A few years ago, I attended York Technical College, where I completed two years of Cisco training with the intention of shifting to the tech industry. But for some reason, I stayed in the factory industry, even though the pull toward technology never truly went away.

Fast-forward to today, and I’ve found my true calling in the field of cybersecurity, network administration, and programming. While I didn't act on the transition back then, it planted a seed that would eventually sprout in the most unexpected way.

Overcoming Doubts and Embracing Technology

Returning to school at 56 wasn’t without its challenges and concerns. The most significant worry was whether I’d be able to break into a new field at my age. Would I be competitive enough for employers, especially in a field dominated by younger, tech-savvy individuals? It was a daunting thought, but I reminded myself to remain hopeful and focused on the skills I was acquiring.

One concern I initially had was the format of modern education. With online courses being a big part of my current schooling, I wasn’t sure how I’d adapt to this style of learning. However, I’ve discovered that online classes actually suit me quite well. The flexibility of learning at my own pace, revisiting lectures, and accessing multiple instructors’ perspectives has been a game-changer. This has made the whole experience easier and more effective than I anticipated.

The Role of Family Support

While I did have fears about making this leap, my family and friends were supportive—if not a little surprised. I was lucky enough to have saved enough money to attend school without the need for work, which has been an incredible blessing. My daughter and grandson live with me, and although looking after my grandson is a time-consuming responsibility, having their support has been invaluable throughout this journey.

Personal Growth: Becoming Smarter with Age

Going back to school at 56 has not only been an academic challenge but a personal one as well. I have far exceeded my own expectations, maintaining a 4.0 GPA, something I couldn’t have imagined when I was younger. Back then, I didn’t invest the time and energy into truly mastering material. Now, I spend hours outside of class reading, listening to extra lectures, and diving deep into subjects I am passionate about—especially cybersecurity.

The growth I’ve experienced has been more than academic. I feel smarter and more adaptable than I did five or ten years ago. My brain is still sharp, and learning new skills, whether programming, databases, or even woodworking, has made me feel more confident in my abilities.

The Unexpected Joys

Beyond academic achievement, going back to college has allowed me to meet new people and experience things that I hadn’t anticipated. It's reinvigorated my curiosity, and I find myself more intellectually engaged than I have been in years. While I was initially unsure about my ability to succeed, the reality has been far better than I expected.

I have also realized that learning isn't confined to the classroom. I’ve learned valuable skills that have already started benefiting me, and I feel ready to transition into a career in technology.

Looking to the Future

My long-term career goals now revolve around using the skills I’m acquiring to help businesses manage their IT infrastructure and protect against cyber threats. Cybersecurity, network administration, and programming are all fields in high demand, and I’m excited about the opportunities ahead. Though I sometimes worry about how my age may be perceived by potential employers, I am determined to remain hopeful and continue moving forward.

Advice for Others Considering Education Later in Life

If you’re reading this and wondering if it’s too late for you to go back to school, my answer is simple: it’s never too late. Yes, there will be challenges—there will always be challenges. But the rewards far outweigh the obstacles. Take the leap, make the jump, and go for it. Life is short, but it isn’t over until it’s over. Education is one of the most empowering tools you can use to reshape your future, no matter how old you are.

In conclusion, my journey back to college at 56 has been one of reinvention, perseverance, and personal growth. It hasn’t been easy, but it has been incredibly rewarding. If you’re on the fence about going back to school, know that it’s never too late to follow your dreams. The only limit is the one you place on yourself.
